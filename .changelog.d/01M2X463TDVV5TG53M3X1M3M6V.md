### Fixed

- **An approved tool call now runs under `running <tool>…` instead of finishing out its execution under `awaiting permission… 0s`** — board item `01M2X463TDVV5TG53M3X1M3M6V`, the third in the row with `01M2V60KWK9AYX3J7V5TPJZN7Q` and `01M2VDD6MQG7H73AHGX8HJGV2J`, and the only one of the three about *which rung is displayed* rather than which clock it reads. A permission prompt replaces the running-tool rung; approving it stopped the wait clock but left the rung itself in place, so for the whole of the call the operator had just approved the status bar claimed conway was blocked on them — with a figure frozen at `0s`, because stopping that clock is exactly what the sibling fix got right. The rung is now restored when the call actually starts executing: `ToolCallStarted` is emitted only after the permission decision allowed the call and after it has a concurrency permit, so it means "executing", not "allowed and queued". The elapsed figure is re-stamped there too — it measures the execution, never the deliberation that preceded it, and the wait is already recorded separately (`waited_ms` on the decision record, and the `allowed once · waited 53s` note).

- **A denied call no longer leaves `awaiting permission…` up either.** A denial is the last event that call produces — no start, no finish — so the rung it left behind used to stay up until the next turn. It now drops to `idle`, which is where the event stream stood before the call was proposed, and the follow-up turn the denial provokes moves it on. A sibling call still executing in the same batch keeps the rung it named: denying one call says nothing about the others.
